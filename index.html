About the CME
56%of Indian patients with chronic kidney disease are malnourished.

Malnutrition in patients with chronic kidney disease is an under-addressed determinant of clinical outcomes, with a pooled prevalence of 56% in Indian patients. This is assessed and managed inconsistently in routine dialysis care. Structured teaching on nutritional assessment and management remains limited across most nephrology and postgraduate training curricula, despite its impact on hospitalization, quality of life, and survival. This program has been designed to equip nephrologists, physicians, and dietitians with a structured, evidence-based approach to nutritional assessment and management of ESRD. This program is endorsed by the Indian Society of Nephrology (ISN) and the IAPEN India Association for Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition.

Endorsed by ISN and IAPEN India

Programme highlights

  1. Comprehensive coverage of nutritional assessment tools.
  2. Panel and case-based discussions on practical clinical dilemmas.
  3. A dedicated hands-on workshop on nutritional assessment and diet planning in pediatric and adolescent ESRD.
  4. Guidance on designing a South Indian renal diet, including food-label literacy and fluid management.
  5. Sessions on emerging areas: plant-based diets in hemodialysis, sarcopenia and frailty, and the dialysis–nutrition–clearance relationship.

Malnutrition in hemodialysis; malnutrition-inflammation-atherosclerosis (MIA) complex syndrome, lipid abnormalities, prevalence, and Indian perspective

Didactic lecture

Dr Avinash Ignatius
Senior Consultant Nephrologist and HOD, Department of Nephrology, Noble Hospitals and Research Centre, Pune

Learning points

The Role of Advanced Body Composition Analysis (BIA, DXA, and Imaging-CT, MRI, POCUS ) in Evaluating Nutrition in ESRD

Didactic lecture

Dr PS Priyamvada
Professor and HOD, Department of Nephrology, JIPMER, Puducherry

Learning points
  • A brief summary of the technical aspects of each modality and their utility in ESRD.
  • Concept of BIA-derived phase angle as an independent prognostic marker of cell membrane integrity and nutritional status, distinct from lean mass alone.
  • Compare the accuracy, reproducibility, cost, and availability of each modality for detecting sarcopenia and tracking nutritional trends over time.
  • Which of these modalities can be adopted for routine clinical practice in the Indian context, versus which remain research tools?
3:25 PM

Dietary Assessment in ESRD: From Clinical History to Objective Protein Kinetics (nPNA)

Didactic lecture

Dr AJ Hemamalini
Professor and HOD, Clinical Nutrition, Sri Ramachandra Institute of Higher Education and Research, Porur, Chennai

Learning points
  • How to take a structured dietary history on dialysis and non-dialysis days using methods like Recall, FFQ, and diary methods, or automated software, and know the pros and cons of each method with examples.
  • How to accurately measure a food portion size -a cup, spoon, katori, or bowl.
  • Role of objective kinetic method like normalised Protein Nitrogen Appearance (nPNA) to estimate dietary protein intake in anuric ESRD, and how to calculate it. and its limitations.

Department of Nephrology, JIPMER

The Department of Nephrology at JIPMER, established in January 2010, is deeply committed to patient care, training and research. The department offers comprehensive and state-of-the-art patient care services, including Clinical Nephrology, Interventional Nephrology, Critical Care Nephrology, Dialysis, and Living and Deceased Donor Kidney Transplantation. It provides structured training for Nephrology Trainees and Dialysis technology students.

The department's mission is to offer evidence-based, ethical, socially mindful patient care emphasising quality and safety, formulate innovations that will serve as a model for training in Nephrology, and engage in socially accountable original research relevant to regional and national health priorities.
